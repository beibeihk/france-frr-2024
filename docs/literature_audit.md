# Verified literature and source audit

Audited 2026-10-03 (Asia/Hong_Kong), before inspecting project outcome data. This is a focused literature audit, not a claimed exhaustive systematic review. Eleven distinct, directly relevant studies were verified. The bibliography table is `references_verified.csv`.

`verified=true` means title, author, date and publication identity were corroborated against a primary source and, for journal papers, publisher-deposited Crossref metadata retrieved directly from `api.crossref.org/works/{DOI}`. It does **not** assert that every journal final PDF was accessible. The precise text version read is recorded below. `used_in_paper=false` is deliberate: there is no final manuscript to inspect at this stage; the paper writer must set it to true only for references actually cited. Empty HAL_ID fields mean unverified or unnecessary identifiers, not missing publications.

## Metadata and access issues resolved

- For ten journal publications, Crossref was queried by exact DOI and returned matching titles/authors/journals. For the one spatial-spillover preprint, the actual arXiv record supplies its DataCite DOI and revision history.
- Behaghel et al. has a 2013 SSRN DOI as well as its 2015 journal DOI. The CSV uses the **journal DOI**, `10.1016/j.jpubeco.2015.03.006`.
- Mayer et al. was published online in 2015 but appears in the 2017 journal issue. The CSV uses 2017 with the publisher's explicit issue evidence.
- Crossref DOI `10.1162/REST_a_00039` resolves to the same lower-case canonical form in the CSV.
- Publisher access failures were recorded instead of being relabelled full-text reads. Open institutional versions do not justify importing numerical final-paper findings without comparing versions.
- No paid database, CASD access, school VPN, author request or commercial microdata was used. Some cited studies used restricted data themselves; that is a feature of prior literature and supplies no authorisation or data source for this project's public-data-only analysis.

## Usable synthesis and remaining contribution test

Previous French work establishes that fiscal zoning has heterogeneous outcomes and that measured establishment attraction can partly represent geographical redistribution. Rural ZRR and urban ZFU are different policy packages. The 2024 FRR reform therefore offers a relevant new policy episode, but its contribution depends on reconstructing **new** territorial eligibility, validating an entry measure from public administrative records, and distinguishing the contrast across boundaries from regional net entry.

Avoid claims that this is the first French rural place-based evaluation, that prior French enterprise-zone evidence uniformly shows success/failure, or that observed SIRENE entries identify job creation or national net entrepreneurship. The older rural literature is a mandatory comparison; French ZFU displacement is a mandatory caveat. The open-data reconstruction itself can be a reproducibility contribution, conditional on audit success.

Before final submission, citations actually used in the manuscript must be checked against the final sentence they support; update `used_in_paper` and document any WP/final version differences. This initial bibliography audit is not the completed four-review publication gate.
